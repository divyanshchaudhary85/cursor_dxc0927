About this model

This is an interactive 3D model of a 765 kV transmission corridor with two parallel single-circuit lines connecting Substation A and Substation B, each carrying a scheduled power flow that you control.

Each line's thermal ampacity limit is computed live from the conductor heat balance used in IEEE Std 738 (Calculating the Current-Temperature Relationship of Bare Overhead Conductors):

I² · R(Tc) + qs = qc + qr

  • qc — convective cooling (depends on wind speed & angle to the line)
  • qr — radiative cooling (depends on conductor & ambient temperature)
  • qs — solar heating (depends on irradiance)

The simulator solves for the maximum current that keeps the conductor at its rated maximum temperature given current weather — this is exactly what a real-time Dynamic Line Rating (DLR) system does, and it is why a line's safe loading can rise on a cold, windy day and fall sharply on a hot, still, sunny one.

Conductor temperature also drives a simplified thermal-sag model: hotter conductors elongate and sag more, reducing ground clearance — another real-world reason utilities limit loading in hot weather.

Conductor and tower parameters are representative, illustrative values for a 4-bundle ACSR 765 kV line. This tool is for education/visualization, not a certified engineering rating study.
